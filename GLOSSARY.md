# Airport Investment Intelligence

Analysts at a US airport-modernisation investor ask which airports would profit most from renovations that add flight and passenger capacity. This glossary fixes the meaning of the terms the agent and its answers use.

## Places

**New England**:
The six states ME, NH, VT, MA, RI and CT, plus Albany (ALB) as a catchment airport for western Massachusetts and Vermont. Answers always label ALB as catchment.
_Avoid_: Northeast, FAA New England region

**LA**:
Los Angeles International Airport (LAX) alone, when a question names "LA" or "Los Angeles" as one airport.
_Avoid_: LA area, Los Angeles airports

**LA basin**:
The five commercial airports serving greater Los Angeles: LAX, BUR, LGB, ONT and SNA. Used only when a question asks about the region or market rather than "LA".
_Avoid_: LA, Southern California

## Traffic

**Long-haul flight**:
A flight departure whose segment great-circle distance is at least 3,000 statute miles.
_Avoid_: international flight, widebody flight

**Long-haul share**:
Long-haul flights as a percentage of all departures performed from an airport in a period, stated for all flights and separately for passenger and cargo flights.
_Avoid_: long-haul percentage of passengers, long-haul capacity share

**Load factor**:
Passengers carried divided by seats offered on passenger flights in a period.
_Avoid_: occupancy, utilisation

**Congestion**:
How badly an airport's departures are held up, measured from observed historical delays rather than runway or gate capacity, and never from today's live status.
_Avoid_: capacity utilisation, busyness

**Unmet demand**:
The seat gap: extra seats an airport's routes would need so that none runs above the target load factor of 80%. Always an estimate, because demand turned away by full flights is never observed.
_Avoid_: latent demand, spilled passengers, lost demand

**Data period**:
The months a figure covers, stated per source: route traffic covers the latest 12 complete months, congestion the latest single month, and growth the last full calendar year against the one before.
_Avoid_: as-of date, fetch date

## Scoring

**National peer set**:
All US commercial-service airports; every KPI is ranked as a percentile against this set, whatever subset a question asks about.
_Avoid_: comparison set, benchmark group

**Terminal-expansion candidate**:
An airport scoring high on three pillars: Growth (passenger growth over the last calendar year), Pressure (how full and congested current capacity is) and Scale (passengers carried).
_Avoid_: investment target, opportunity

**Congestion index**:
A 0–100 measure of congestion: the mean national percentile of the share of departures delayed 15 minutes or more, the mean departure delay and the mean taxi-out time. Delay causes explain it but do not count towards it.
_Avoid_: delay score, congestion score

**Expansion score**:
The 0–100 ranking of a terminal-expansion candidate: Growth 40%, Pressure 40%, Scale 20%, each pillar a national percentile. Pressure is the mean of the load-factor percentile and the congestion index.
_Avoid_: investment score, opportunity score
